> **Epistemic status**: TSCG is **not a theory of reality**. It is a toolkit with
> a pedagogical vocation; its Layer Cake (M3–M1) describes the kit itself — the
> rules that keep the layers coherent and let scale models be built and checked
> (`LayerCake_Models_The_Kit.md`).
>
> **What this document assumes.** The epistemological stance is stated once, in
> `ContextSwitching.md`, and only recalled here. Every paradigm rests on something
> it does not demonstrate — under every Discworld, a Great A'Tuin. A paradigm is
> coherent **on its own criteria**, which a maquette **declares**; an M0 instance is
> a maquette built within one context — any coherent paradigm, scientific or not —
> and TSCG never says which paradigm is true. Moving from one context to another is
> a **context switch**.
>
> This document describes a *candidate* M0 instance type that models a switch of a
> particular kind: **the paradigm shift** — the passage from one paradigm to another,
> as a community lived it — refutation, bounding, or restoration — **admitted only
> when that change has already been recorded outside TSCG** by the history of
> culture, of which the history of science is one branch among others. A switch, in
> `ContextSwitching.md`, is **individual, willing and reversible**; a supersession is
> **collective, historical and mostly one-way**. The second presupposes the first.
>
> TSCG renders no verdict and offers no theory of paradigm change; it records
> institutional consensus as a dated, sourced historical fact, never as a truth
> claim. The type is not sealed in any ontology file; its relation typology rests on
> a handful of paper analyses, and the open questions (Section 10) are the rigorous
> part, not the leftover.
>
> The same rule binds TSCG itself: **TSCG can be neither self-validated nor
> self-refuted.** Its vocation is to be a *revisable proposal* — a toolkit
> introducing newcomers to systemic modelling — and the judgment of that proposal
> belongs to others.

## 1. The Inversion

Every M0 instance in the corpus is a maquette built *inside* one context: a fire
read through combustion chemistry, a cell read through molecular biology, a myth
read through its own internal logic. The modeller chooses the subject, and chooses
it because the kit can build it. Nothing in such a maquette says how its paradigm
came to be held, or what it replaced.

A Model Supersession inverts the stance. Its subject is not chosen by the modeller
for its fit; it is chosen **because the world has already recorded it** — an
episode in which a community moved from one paradigm to another, *before and
independently of TSCG*. Geocentrism gave way to heliocentrism; the one-way central
dogma gave way to bidirectional flow between nucleic acids; continental drift was
rejected, then restored.

Such a maquette does not say which paradigm is right — the stance of
`ContextSwitching.md` forbids it. It lays out, with the kit's pieces, **how a
paradigm stops being held** (and, occasionally, how it comes back), using changes
that TSCG did not decide and does not explain. The kit builds the maquette; the
record of the change comes from outside.

This document has two companions. `ContextSwitching.md` describes the switch one
person can make, willingly and reversibly, between coherent contexts; this document
describes the switch a whole community made, as history records it. And
`The_Epistemic_Residue.md` describes the part of the evidence a paradigm does not
absorb, *before* any verdict; this document starts where the verdict falls, once a
shift is on record. Read together with the residue, it divides one episode by its
tense.

## 2. Escaping Autarky: External Arbitration

### 2.1 The rejected formulation

An earlier idea — *a refutation framework **for** TSCG* — was rejected as
**disguised autarky**. It treated TSCG as a theory awaiting refutation, and made
TSCG both the object under examination and the judge. No refinement of the
criterion rescues that structure: the kit would grade its own copy. TSCG can be
neither self-validated nor self-refuted; as a toolkit, it is judged by others —
by whether it helps them build and understand — not by a protocol of its own.

### 2.2 The dissolution

The inversion of Section 1 does not *manage* this risk; it removes it at the root.
When the paradigm shift is **recorded outside TSCG**, the verdict is
sub-contracted to the world, and the kit has no hand on it:

- phlogiston was abandoned after Lavoisier's oxygen chemistry, before TSCG existed;
- the luminiferous ether was abandoned after the Michelson–Morley null result and
  special relativity;
- geocentrism was displaced by heliocentric astronomy, from Copernicus to Newton.

TSCG arrives *after*, as a late observer of a closed episode. It **cannot rig a
verdict history has already rendered.** What remains is ordinary modelling bias —
*which* episodes are chosen, *how* they are modelled (the constitutive δ₁ of any
Map). That bias is real and is handled by the usual means (multisubjective
evaluation, credibility accretion), but it never reaches the verdict itself.

A word on vocabulary. *Paradigm shift* is Kuhn's term (1962); Kuhn reserved it
for scientific revolutions, while this document uses it in a broader sense — any
recorded passage from one paradigm to the next, the kind of passage being specified
by the relation typology (Section 5). When this document says a model was
*refuted*, it reports a historical fact — the community abandoned it, for reasons
historians document — and not a judgment by TSCG that the model is false. Every
paradigm stands on its turtle (`ContextSwitching.md`, §1): the abandoned paradigm
remains as legitimate a subject for a maquette as the one that replaced it.

### 2.3 The arbitration mode

A type is defined by **how its instances are arbitrated**, not by the intention
behind them. The arbitration mode of a Model Supersession is
**`externally_established`**: the record of the shift belongs to the history of
culture and is independent of TSCG. The relevant branch — history of science, of
religions, of ideas, literary or cultural history — is simply the one whose
historiography records the shift; it is read from the source, not assigned by a
label (Section 3.5).

This is what separates it from the instance types on HEAD. A Poclet, a
TransDisclet, a RealWorldSystem or a CaseStudy is a maquette of a *system*; a
SystemicFramework maps an *existing methodology*; a SymbolicSystemGrammar models a
*symbolic system* whose value lies in its interpretations; a TscgTool operates *on*
TSCG artifacts. Each is built within one context. None of them is a maquette of a
**paradigm shift** whose verdict was rendered elsewhere.

### 2.4 The admission rule

The load-bearing word is **recorded** (attested). One rule follows, stated once
and applied at the door:

> **Any coherent paradigm may be modelled. A paradigm shift is modelled only when
> the history of culture has already recorded it.**
> An open controversy has no recorded verdict; the competing paradigms may each
> have their maquette, but no supersession between them is modelled.

The rule bears on the **supersession**, never on the paradigms. Dark matter, string
theory, the flat Earth or the Discworld can each be the subject of a maquette; what
TSCG will not do is model one of them *replacing* another before historians have
recorded that it did.

This is a *sorting rule*, applied when a candidate seed is admitted (Section 7) —
not an attribute stored on each instance. An attribute that every admitted instance
must carry with the same value says nothing (Section 11).

The same arbiter extends one level down. Whether a paradigm *was the institutional
consensus* at a given time is itself a historical fact, read from the
historiography — never decided by TSCG (Section 4). Where historians
disagree on whether a consensus existed, the case is quarantined too.

### 2.5 Revisability without a flag

"Already recorded" does not mean "final". Science revises its verdicts, and
historians revise their accounts. TSCG does not mark each instance as revisable:
revisability is a framework-wide axiom, stated once (the WS-8 *Until Further
Notice* entry), and holds everywhere. When a verdict is revised, the revision is
simply **a new supersession on the next Milestone** of the same paradigm history —
the continental-drift case is the textbook example: rejection, then restoration
(Sections 3 and 5).

## 3. What an Instance Models

### 3.1 A paradigm shift, not a paradigm

An ordinary M0 instance is a maquette built within one context. A Model
Supersession is a maquette of something that *happened between* paradigms: the
moment a community stopped holding one and started holding another. Its object is
that **supersession**, not the subject matter either paradigm is about. A Model
Supersession on the central dogma is not a model of molecular biology; it is a
model of how molecular biology's account of information flow changed.

This gives the instance an unusual layering, stated plainly rather than smoothed
over:

- the **territory** of the maquette is a historical episode, as recorded by the
  history of culture;
- the **components** of that episode are paradigms — themselves *maps*, ways of
  knowing, each of which could be the subject of its own maquette.

A Model Supersession is therefore a map of a territory made of maps. This is
consistent with the Map–Territory distinction, not a breach of it: a paradigm is
characterised on the Map and Stereopsis side (Gm | Gs), because it is a way of
*knowing*, not a thing in the world; the shift between paradigms is what the
maquette reduces.

### 3.2 The building blocks

The July design (SC-7, not graved) is kept, with one renaming: what July called
`Paradigm` — the ordered line — is now **ParadigmHistory**, so that *paradigm*
keeps its ordinary, Kuhnian sense.

| Piece | What it is | Role in the maquette |
|---|---|---|
| **Paradigm** | One coherent way of modelling a part of reality (geocentrism, heliocentrism, the one-way central dogma…), on its own criteria | What a community holds or abandons; may be the subject of its own M0 maquette |
| **ParadigmHistory** | The succession of paradigms on one question, through time (e.g. "what is at the centre of the cosmos?") | Holds an **ordered sequence of Milestones**; the order *is* the history |
| **Milestone** | One paradigm at one ordered position of a ParadigmHistory | The unit a supersession connects; carries the institutional consensus of that position (Section 4) and the declared criteria of its paradigm (Section 3.5) |
| **ModelSupersession** | The change from one Milestone to the next | Links `fromStateOfTheArt` → `toStateOfTheArt` (both of range Milestone); records the `anomaly`, the `supersessionRelation` and, when there is one, the `survivingInvariant` (Section 5) |
| **anomaly** | The externally recorded observation or result that triggered the change | Reverse transcriptase; the Michelson–Morley null result; Gödel's theorems |

Three consequences of this layout carry the design.

**Milestone is not Paradigm.** A Milestone is a *position* in a history; a Paradigm
is a *way of knowing*. Keeping them apart lets the same paradigm appear at two
positions — which is exactly what a restoration is: a paradigm set aside, then
restored (continental drift, Section 6). It also lets the consensus be dated per
position, since a paradigm can be the consensus at one time and not at another.

**One history, not two models.** A supersession is not a pair of free-standing
objects, "the old model" and "the new model". It is **one** paradigm history
crossing a milestone. The central dogma is one such history — the account of
sequential information flow between nucleic acids and proteins — crossing one
milestone, from *one-way flow* to *bidirectional flow between nucleic acids, none
out of protein*. Keeping both states inside one history preserves the continuity
that bounding depends on.

**The order is carried by the sequence.** Because Milestones are ordered within
their ParadigmHistory, "before" and "after" need no extra marker. The current state
of the art is the Milestone whose institutional-consensus period is still open
(Section 4); it is inferred, not stored.

### 3.3 Composition from existing pieces

No new M2 generic concept is needed. The two concepts the type exhibits compose
from M2 concepts already on HEAD, via the naming function `Fm2`:

- **Milestone** = `Fm2(Identity, Trajectory)` — what endures, located at an ordered
  point of its path;
- **EpistemicMorphism** = `Fm2(Modelisation, Transformation)` — a change *between*
  maps, under the pressure of new information. A Model Supersession is the
  externally recorded case of this concept.

Both combos were staged in July (SC-0) and never committed; their expanded
formulas, typing and placement are settled at graving (SC-7), under the conventions
on HEAD, not here.

### 3.4 Domain

The domain of a Model Supersession is the **history of culture** — a transversal
domain — not the discipline the paradigms belong to; the branch that attests a
given shift (history of science, of religions, of ideas…) is the one whose
historiography records it (Section 2.3). The central-dogma instance does not plug
into Biology: its object is the paradigm shift, not the biology. How that domain is
declared follows the current domain model on HEAD (domains as classes, SC-5) and is
settled at graving.

### 3.5 Declared criteria — one context per maquette

`ContextSwitching.md` (§3.5) asks every maquette to **declare its criteria** — the
turtle it is built on — rather than a label for the *kind* of paradigm it stands in.
A Model Supersession applies that rule at two levels.

- **The maquette's own context.** A supersession maquette stands in one context: the
  history of culture, whose criterion is **historiographic attestation**. That is
  what Sections 2 and 4 already say; declaring it makes it explicit.
- **The criteria of each Milestone.** Each Milestone records the criteria its
  paradigm judged itself by — agreement with naked-eye positional observation for
  Ptolemaic astronomy, experiment for the central dogma, finitary proof for Hilbert's
  programme, a credible mechanism as well as geological evidence for the fixists who
  rejected Wegener. The criteria say what made the paradigm coherent, and therefore
  where its residue lay (`The_Epistemic_Residue.md`).

Three rules keep the declaration honest.

- **The criteria are attested, not judged.** Like institutional consensus
  (Section 4), they come from a source and record **what the paradigm claims for
  itself**. Whether the claim is borne out is not decided by the declaration. String
  theory presents itself as physics open to test; its criteria are recorded *as
  claimed*, and the debate over its testability stays open.
- **The criteria explain; they do not rank.** No set of criteria is closer to truth
  than another. A maquette built on mythological criteria and one built on
  experimental criteria use the same pieces and are judged by the same standard:
  internal coherence on their declared criteria, usefulness as a maquette. This is
  the reason the earlier proposal — a facet labelling the *kind* of paradigm — was
  withdrawn: a label classifies without explaining, and quietly ranks (Section 11).
- **One context per maquette; every switch declared.** A maquette stands in one
  context. When a subject crosses contexts — a paradigm that borrows the authority of
  one context to validate the claims of another, *the existence of God proven by
  science*, *humanism read through a religious prism* — the crossing is declared as
  a switch, not fused into a single, undeclared set of criteria. The same holds for a
  ParadigmHistory: when the criteria themselves change across a shift — which, after
  Kuhn, is common — the change is recorded on the supersession as part of what
  happened, not smoothed over.

How criteria are declared formally — an extension of the `observer` field that
several M0 instances already carry, a short text in the instance README, or
something else — is **not settled** (`ContextSwitching.md`, §13), and is not to be
graved before it pays for itself by use. In this document, the criteria given in
Sections 6 and 7 are short paper entries, not a format.

## 4. Institutional Consensus — "Until Further Notice"

### 4.1 What it records

A paradigm history records not only *which* paradigm replaced which, but *who held
which, and when*. That second record is needed to read the first. Three cases show
why:

- **Geocentrism** was the institutional consensus for centuries, then was refuted.
- **Newtonian mechanics** was the consensus, then was bounded — still taught, within
  a delimited domain.
- **Continental drift** was *never* the consensus in Wegener's lifetime: the
  consensus of that period was its rejection. Decades later, plate tectonics
  restored mobilism and became the consensus.

Each Milestone therefore carries an attribute, **`institutionalConsensus`**, which
records — as a dated **historical fact of the Map**, not a truth claim — whether
and when the paradigm at that position was the institutional consensus. The
attribute is meaningful even when the paradigm later proves wrong, and it may be
absent: a Milestone can hold a paradigm that was proposed, recorded and debated
without ever being the consensus (Wegener's drift).

### 4.2 Structure

`institutionalConsensus` has three parts:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Period** | When the paradigm was the consensus — a start, and an end if there is one |
| **Holder** | Who held the consensus, in the terms of that discipline and era |
| **Source** | The historiographic source that attests it |

The holder is described *in the terms of its own time and field*: the community
that held geocentrism is not described with the institutions of twentieth-century
geology, and vice versa.

### 4.3 "Until further notice" is an open period

The motto of the epigraph is carried by the data structure, not by a flag. The
current state of the art is the Milestone whose consensus period **has no end
date**. Nothing else marks it as provisional: an open period *is* "until further
notice". When a later shift closes that period, the record does not change in
meaning — it gains an end date, and the next Milestone opens its own period.

The date at which an instance was last recorded is ordinary instance metadata
(`dcterms:modified`), not a property of the consensus.

### 4.4 No general definition of "institution"

What counts as an institution varies by discipline and by era: a university
curriculum, a reference textbook, a learned journal, an academy, a prize, a
standards body, a religious authority. A universal definition would be a
miniature unifying theory of knowledge — exactly what TSCG does not offer. The rule
is therefore written once:

> **Institutional consensus is attested by a historiographic source; TSCG gives no
> general definition of "institution".**

The observable markers that the source relies on — curricula, textbooks, reference
journals, academies, prizes, standards — are Map facts: on the REVOI side, they are
what makes the consensus *Observable* and *Verifiable*.

### 4.5 Not TSCG's verdict

The arbiter is the historiography — the branch of the history of culture that
records the shift (Section 2.3). `externally_established` applies to the consensus
itself as much as to the shift (Section 2.4): TSCG does not decide that a community
held a paradigm; it reports that historians say so, and cites them. Where
historians disagree on whether a consensus existed — or on when it began or ended —
the case is quarantined rather than settled by the modeller.

### 4.6 "Dogma" belongs in prose only

The word *dogma* is not used as a value or a label. Calling a consensus a dogma
would be a judgment on the community that held it — TSCG would become a judge — and
it would collide with the Central Dogma, a seed of this type whose name is
historical, not pejorative.

The word survives **in prose**, to name a pathology: a consensus that *immunizes
itself* against anything that could revise it — Popper's immunizing stratagem,
Lakatos's degenerating research programme (see `The_Epistemic_Residue.md`, §2). In
the terms of `ContextSwitching.md` (§7.4), it is a community **locked in one
context**: what makes a consensus a dogma is not that it is wrong, but that it is no
longer held in a way that allows a switch. That pathology is a matter for
commentary, never for an attribute.

### 4.7 Encoding: deferred, reuse before inventing

How the period is encoded — dedicated start/end properties, or an existing
vocabulary such as the W3C *Time Ontology in OWL* (OWL-Time) — is settled at
graving (SC-7), following the Bootstrap rule *reuse before inventing*. Two
constraints are fixed here: an open period must be expressible without an
artificial end date, and imprecise dates (a consensus that formed over decades)
must be recorded as the source gives them, not sharpened by the modeller.

## 5. The Relation Typology

### 5.1 Four relations

Every supersession records **how** the earlier paradigm fared. Four relations
were found on the July seeds:

| Relation | What the record shows | Seeds |
|---|---|---|
| `refutation` | The earlier paradigm was abandoned; nothing of it survives in the next | geocentrism, the luminiferous ether, phlogiston |
| `boundingByMorphism` | The earlier paradigm survives, unchanged, as a delimited case of the next, and is recovered from it by a structure-preserving map (a limit, a subset, a restriction) | Newton → special relativity (limit); the central dogma (subset) |
| `boundingByImpossibility` | A reduced part of the earlier paradigm survives, but its over-claim was shown impossible; no map recovers the original | Hilbert's programme → Gödel |
| `restoration` | A paradigm set aside earlier returns as the consensus | continental drift → plate tectonics |

As with consensus, the relation is **read from the record**, with its sources: the
correspondence between Newtonian and relativistic mechanics, or the scope of
Gödel's theorems, are established in the fields themselves. TSCG classifies what
the record shows; it does not add a verdict to it. And since every paradigm stands
on its turtle, none of the four says which paradigm is *true* — *refutation*
reports an abandonment, *bounding* a delimitation, *restoration* a return.

### 5.2 Two axes, plus a return

The first three relations are generated by two independent questions.

- **Axis 1 — does the earlier paradigm survive in the next?**
  No → `refutation`. Yes → bounding.
- **Axis 2 — if it survives, is it recovered by a structure-preserving map?**
  Yes → `boundingByMorphism`. No → `boundingByImpossibility`.

`restoration` is of another kind: not a fate of the earlier paradigm but a
*return* of an even earlier one. The layout of Section 3 makes it directly
readable: since a Milestone is a position and not a paradigm, a restoration is a
shift whose `toStateOfTheArt` holds a paradigm that already appeared at an earlier
position of the same history, without consensus or with a consensus since closed.

Both bounding sub-relations recur across unrelated fields — by morphism in physics
and biology (and, beyond the July seeds, classical → quantum mechanics, Euclidean →
non-Euclidean geometry); by impossibility in logic (and perpetual motion →
thermodynamics). The split is a structural axis, not an artifact of one case.

### 5.3 The discriminator: `survivingInvariant`

Axis 1 is carried by one attribute of the supersession, `survivingInvariant`: the
part of the earlier paradigm that the next one preserves.

- **Present** → bounding. Newtonian mechanics survives as the low-velocity regime;
  Crick's invariant — no sequential information flows *out of* protein — survives
  the discovery of reverse transcriptase; the power of formalisation survives Gödel.
- **Absent** → refutation. Nothing of phlogiston survives in oxygen chemistry as a
  delimited case.

The attribute reuses an existing piece: an invariant, in the M2 sense on HEAD, is a
*property unchanged under specific transformations* — here, the transformation is
the paradigm shift itself.

Isaac Asimov's essay *The Relativity of Wrong* (1989) puts the bounding case in a
sentence worth citing in prose: a paradigm that is bounded was not simply wrong; it
was *less wrong* than its predecessors, and its successor is less wrong still.

### 5.4 Signatures

Read with the kit's pieces, each relation leaves a distinct structural signature.
This table, from the July central-dogma analysis, is what the anti-decorative test
(Section 8) relies on.

| Relation | Signature | Surviving invariant |
|---|---|---|
| `refutation` | **Attractor replacement** — the paradigm the community converged on (A) is abandoned for an incompatible one | none |
| `boundingByMorphism` / `boundingByImpossibility` | **Constraint addition** — a domain boundary (`m2:Constraint`) is added; the earlier paradigm survives inside it | yes |
| `restoration` | **Attractor restoration** — a paradigm set aside returns as the one the community converges on | the restored paradigm's core |

The signature uses only the attractor primitive (A) of the Territory grammar and
the M2 concept `Constraint`; were further primitives needed, they would be written
with their index (`St`/`Ss`, `It`/`Im`).

### 5.5 The four relations as switches

Seen from `ContextSwitching.md`, each relation says what happened to the
**switch** between the two contexts — whether the community, and a learner after it,
can still move from the later paradigm back into the earlier one.

| Relation | The switch back to the earlier context | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `boundingByMorphism` | **Kept open** | The recovery map *is* the saved state: Newton is recovered from relativity as `v ≪ c`, and engineers switch into Newtonian mechanics every day, legitimately, within its domain |
| `boundingByImpossibility` | **Partly open** | A reduced context can still be entered — formalisation, proof theory — but no map returns the original over-claim; Hilbert's complete, self-certifying system cannot be re-entered as a programme |
| `refutation` | **Closed** — for the community | No institution switches back into phlogiston chemistry to do chemistry |
| `restoration` | **Re-opened** | A context set aside becomes the one the community stands in again — mobilism, without Wegener's mechanism |

Two points keep the reading in its place.

- **A closed switch is closed for the community, not for a maquette.** By willing
  suspension of disbelief (`ContextSwitching.md`, §6.3), a historian or a learner can
  still step into phlogiston chemistry, see it coherent on its criteria, and step
  out again. Re-opening for a learner a switch that history has closed is one of the
  things a Model Supersession maquette is for.
- **The reading adds no relation and no attribute.** It re-describes the four
  relations of Section 5.1; the typology itself is unchanged.

### 5.6 Limits of the typology, stated here

- **Morphism and meaning.** The recovery of Newtonian mechanics as the v ≪ c limit
  of special relativity is a formal fact. Kuhn himself contested that this makes
  Newton a *special case* of Einstein, because the concepts (mass, time) change
  meaning across the shift — the incommensurability thesis. The maquette records
  the formal recovery and does not settle the philosophical debate; the debate is
  named in Section 10. In the terms of `ContextSwitching.md` (§11), a switch says
  what it loses; this is what the Newton → relativity switch may lose.
- **Seed base.** The typology was confirmed on seeds whose paradigms judge
  themselves by experiment and observation, and on one whose criterion is proof
  (Hilbert → Gödel) only. Whether the four relations — morphism in particular — have
  counterparts in histories whose paradigms stand on other criteria (religious,
  political, mythological…) is open (Section 10).

## 6. Seeds, Analysed

Paper analyses only — no `.jsonld`. Each seed is laid out with the pieces of
Section 3; consensus periods and dates are given at the resolution the sources
support, and must be re-checked against a cited historiographic source before any
instance is graved. The criteria given for each history are short paper entries
(Section 3.5), not a format.

### 6.1 The central dogma — `boundingByMorphism` (subset)

- **ParadigmHistory**: *how does sequential information flow between nucleic acids
  and proteins?* — criteria: experiment (molecular biology).
- **Milestone 1**: the popular, one-way form — DNA → RNA → protein; RNA cannot make
  DNA. Consensus of textbook molecular biology in the 1960s.
- **Anomaly**: reverse transcriptase, reported independently by Temin and by
  Baltimore (1970) — RNA → DNA in retroviruses.
- **Milestone 2**: information flows in both directions between nucleic acids;
  none flows *out of* protein. Consensus open.
- **survivingInvariant**: no sequential information leaves protein.

The historical precision is the point of the seed. Crick's own statement of the
dogma forbade only transfers *out of* protein, and in his 1970 restatement he
listed RNA → DNA as a possible special transfer. Reverse transcriptase therefore
bounded the *popular over-generalisation*, not Crick's dogma. The earlier
paradigm survives as a subset of the next: the one-way paths are still there; one
more path is allowed. A maquette that lays this out corrects a common muddle — "the
central dogma was overturned" — which a flat narrative leaves in place.

### 6.2 Newton → special relativity — `boundingByMorphism` (limit)

- **ParadigmHistory**: *how do motion, space and time compose?* — criteria:
  experiment and measurement.
- **Milestone 1**: Newtonian mechanics as universal — Galilean velocity addition
  `w = u + v`, absolute time. Consensus from the eighteenth century to the early
  twentieth.
- **Anomaly**: the Michelson–Morley null result (1887), and the failure of Galilean
  transformations to preserve Maxwell's equations.
- **Milestone 2**: special relativity (1905) — Lorentz transformations,
  `w = (u + v) / (1 + uv/c²)`. Consensus open.
- **Recovery map**: as `c → ∞` (equivalently `v ≪ c`), the relativistic addition
  reduces to `u + v`.
- **survivingInvariant**: Newtonian mechanics, unchanged, as the low-velocity
  regime.

The consensus that closed is Newton-*as-universal*; Newton-*as-low-velocity-regime*
is still taught. The two Milestones make that distinction visible without any
extra attribute. (The incommensurability objection is recorded in Section 5.6.)

### 6.3 Hilbert's programme → Gödel — `boundingByImpossibility`

- **ParadigmHistory**: *can mathematics be secured by a complete formal system that
  certifies its own consistency?* — criteria: proof within a formal system; for the
  programme itself, finitary proof.
- **Milestone 1**: Hilbert's programme (1920s) — a consistent formal system rich
  enough for arithmetic can be complete and prove its own consistency by finitary
  means.
- **Anomaly**: Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931).
- **Milestone 2**: formal systems are powerful but intrinsically incomplete and
  not self-certifying. Consensus open.
- **survivingInvariant**: the power of formalisation — most of mathematics is
  formalisable; proof theory flourished; Gentzen (1936) proved the consistency of
  arithmetic, though not within arithmetic and not by finitary means.
- **No recovery map**: no limit, parameter or embedding returns Hilbert's original
  aim. The over-claim was shown impossible; a reduced programme survives.

This is the seed that split bounding in two. It is also the seed that shows why a
label for the *kind* of paradigm would not have done: a mathematical paradigm is
settled by proof, not by experiment, and declaring that criterion says so directly —
including the finer point, invisible to any label, that the criterion at stake was
*finitary* proof, which is exactly what Gentzen's later result gave up.

### 6.4 Three refutations, briefly

- **Geocentrism → heliocentrism.** Criteria: observation and prediction of celestial
  positions — with, until Kepler, the further criterion of uniform circular motion.
  No single anomaly: a sequence — Copernicus (1543), Kepler's ellipses, Galileo's
  telescopic observations (including the phases of Venus, which the Tychonic system
  could still accommodate), closing with Newtonian gravitation (1687). The seed shows
  that `anomaly` may have to be plural, and that the criteria themselves changed
  along the way (Section 3.5). The history continues: heliocentrism was itself later
  bounded — the Sun is the centre of the Solar System, not of the universe.
- **The luminiferous ether.** Criteria: experiment. Anomaly: the Michelson–Morley
  null result; the Lorentz–FitzGerald contraction was proposed to save the ether,
  then special relativity made it superfluous. Nothing of it survives as a delimited
  case.
- **Phlogiston → oxygen chemistry.** Criteria: experiment (combustion, calcination,
  weighing). Anomaly: metals gain weight when calcined. Lavoisier's oxygen chemistry
  replaced it in the late eighteenth century. Some historians argue the abandonment
  was premature, and that parts of phlogiston theory had real content; that debate
  concerns whether the abandonment *was justified*, not whether it *happened*. The
  maquette records the abandonment and cites the debate — it does not adjudicate it.

### 6.5 Continental drift — `refutation`, then `restoration`

- **ParadigmHistory**: *are the continents fixed?* — criteria: geological and
  geophysical evidence, and a credible physical mechanism.
- **Milestone 1**: fixism. Consensus.
- **Milestone 2**: Wegener's continental drift (1912–1915). **No consensus**: the
  consensus of the period was its rejection.
- **Milestone 3**: fixism reaffirmed. Consensus, from the 1920s to the 1950s.
- **Milestone 4**: plate tectonics — seafloor spreading, magnetic anomalies over
  ocean ridges (Vine and Matthews, 1963), plate theory. Consensus from the late
  1960s, open.

Two supersessions:

- **2 → 3, `refutation`**: drift rejected, chiefly for want of a credible mechanism.
- **3 → 4, `restoration`**: mobilism returns as the consensus. **survivingInvariant**:
  *the continents move*. Wegener's mechanism — continents ploughing through oceanic
  crust — is not restored: in plate tectonics, plates ride on mantle convection.

The case is also the cleanest demonstration of why a Milestone is not a Paradigm:
fixism appears twice (Milestones 1 and 3), mobilism twice (2 and 4), and the
consensus is dated per position. And it shows what declaring criteria buys: the
rejection turned on the *mechanism* criterion, which the evidence of fit could not
satisfy; the restoration came with evidence that answered it.

**Three readings of one episode, kept distinct.**

| Document | What it does with Wegener |
|---|---|
| `M0_PlateTectonics.jsonld` (Poclet, on HEAD) | A maquette of the **system** as Milestone 4's paradigm describes it — including the point that plates are passive riders, not self-propelled, which is exactly the part of Wegener's mechanism that was not restored |
| `The_Epistemic_Residue.md` (on HEAD) | Wegener as a **residue before its verdict** — a real correspondence dismissed by the silo specialist |
| This type | The **shifts** themselves, after the verdict: refutation, then restoration |

The three do not overlap: one models a system within a paradigm, one the
residue before the verdict, one the record of the shifts.

## 7. Seed Sorting and Quarantine

### 7.1 Three bins — for shifts, not for paradigms

The admission rule (Section 2.4) sorts **candidate shifts**, never paradigms. Every
paradigm below may have its own maquette; the bins only say whether a
*supersession* can be modelled.

**Bin 1 — Recorded shift → IN.** A paradigm history in which the history of culture
records that one paradigm gave way to another. This is the type's core: all the
seeds of Section 6.

**Bin 2 — No Institutional Consensus → OUT of the type.** A paradigm that was
proposed and debated, but for which no source attests that it was ever the
institutional consensus, and which no recorded shift connects to a successor.
There is no shift to model — so the type does not apply. Two points keep this bin
honest:

- *Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.* "No Institutional Consensus" is
  a statement about the record, not a verdict on the paradigm. TSCG does not say the
  paradigm is false or discredited; it says no consensus is attested. The paradigm
  remains a legitimate subject for an ordinary maquette.
- The bin is not permanent. A paradigm without consensus can enter a paradigm
  history later — exactly as Wegener's drift did, proposed without consensus and
  restored decades afterwards (Section 6.5). What separates Wegener's Milestone 2
  from Bin 2 is that it sits *inside* a history whose other positions carry
  consensus and whose shifts are on record.

Examples: neuro-linguistic programming (proposed in the 1970s; reviews of the
research literature find no empirical support, and no source attests an
institutional consensus in its favour); the flat Earth as a scientific model
(historians attest that the educated consensus held a spherical Earth from
antiquity onwards, and that the "medieval flat Earth" is itself a later myth).

**Bin 3 — Open controversy → no supersession.** Competing paradigms are alive and
the record shows no verdict: dark matter versus modified gravity; string theory.
Each competing paradigm may have its maquette. What TSCG does not model is one of
them *replacing* another, because that would be TSCG rendering a verdict the
history of culture has not rendered. The quarantine is kept in this document as a
**demonstration of the boundary**: these are precisely the cases where a modeller
would be tempted to play judge.

### 7.2 Paradigms with nothing to shift

The Discworld, Amber, psychohistory or the world of *Neuromancer* are perfectly
modellable — each as a maquette whose declared criteria are the rules of its own
world, judged by its internal coherence on them. In general no shift involving them
is recorded, so no supersession applies. Should literary or cultural history ever
record one, the same rule would admit it, within the branch that records it and
with every switch declared (Section 3.5).

| Geocentrism → heliocentrism | observation and prediction of positions | 1 | `refutation` |

## 8. The Anti-Decorative Test

### 8.1 The risk

A standing risk for any instance type is **decorative universality**: restating a
known history in the kit's vocabulary and calling it a model. If a Model
Supersession on phlogiston only retells Lavoisier with ASFID labels, it adds
nothing — and revives the critique "applicable to everything, therefore to
nothing".

### 8.2 The test, reframed

The test is *not* "does TSCG discover a structure of scientific change?" — that
would be a claim about the world, which the kit does not make
(`LayerCake_Models_The_Kit.md`, §4). The test is:

> **Does laying out a shift with the kit's pieces make visible something that a
> flat narrative leaves blurred — so that a newcomer learns to see it?**

### 8.3 What the seeds show so far

- **Refutation versus bounding.** The `survivingInvariant` question forces a
  distinction that "these are all model revisions" hides: phlogiston left nothing
  behind; Newton left a whole regime.
- **Two kinds of bounding.** The presence or absence of a recovery map separates
  Newton → relativity from Hilbert → Gödel — a distinction that did not appear
  until the seeds were laid out side by side.
- **A common muddle corrected.** "The central dogma was overturned" is false; the
  maquette shows which claim was bounded (the popular one-way form) and which
  survived (Crick's invariant).
- **Partial restoration made visible.** Because a Milestone is not a Paradigm, the
  continental-drift maquette shows that mobilism was restored while Wegener's
  mechanism was not.
- **The decisive criterion made visible.** Declaring each history's criteria shows
  *which* criterion a shift turned on — the mechanism, for Wegener; finitary proof,
  for Hilbert — where a flat narrative says only "rejected" or "refuted".
- **The anomaly is not always one event.** Geocentrism has a sequence of anomalies,
  not a single one.

### 8.4 What the seeds do not show

- They show no law of scientific change. The July drafts hoped for a
  "transdisciplinary structure of refutation" revealed across fields; that hope is
  withdrawn as stated. The relations recur across the maquettes — an observation
  about the maquettes, open to revision, not a law about science.
- Coverage is uneven: `refutation` has three seeds from unrelated fields;
  `boundingByMorphism` two; `boundingByImpossibility` and `restoration` one each.
  The July criterion — at least three seeds per relation, from unrelated fields,
  before the type is sealed — is not yet met for the last three.

## 10. The Shore

> *"As our island of knowledge grows, so does the shore of our ignorance."*
> — John Archibald Wheeler

As in `The_Epistemic_Residue.md`, the open questions are the rigorous part.

**Morphism and meaning.** The formal recovery of Newton inside relativity is a
fact; Kuhn's incommensurability thesis denies that it makes Newton a special case
of Einstein. The maquette records the formula and leaves the debate open.

**Beyond experiment and proof.** The typology was confirmed on seeds whose criteria
are experiment and observation, and on one whose criterion is proof. Do shifts
recorded in the history of religions, of ideas or of myths show refutation,
bounding, restoration — and what would a "recovery map" be there? Not known; no such
seed has been laid out.

**How criteria are declared.** The principle is stated (Section 3.5); its form is
not — an extended `observer` field, README text, or something else
(`ContextSwitching.md`, §13). The short criteria entries of Sections 6 and 7 are
paper placeholders. Whether a supersession should also record, as such, a *change of
criteria* across a shift — or whether that stays in prose — is open.

**Anticipation.** When a work of fiction comes to resemble a later paradigm — the
word *cyberspace* before the thing — that is not a supersession: treating it as one
would be an undeclared switch between a literary context and a technical one.
Whether it deserves a relation of its own is a door, marked, not opened.

**Bounding and the Stereopsis grammar.** A bounding redraws the domain over which
the earlier paradigm still holds. Whether a Stereopsis primitive carries that, and
which, is open. `L` (Localizability) is a tempting candidate, but not in the sense its
name suggests: on HEAD, `L` is the ordinal discrimination of convergence toward an
Attractor ("Converging?") — an attractor located in a space of states (a phase space,
a fitness landscape), not in physical space. The M3 rejects the spatial reading, and
the WS-3 L-cluster addendum traces several misattributions to it. Read in its M3
sense, `L` would compare successive paradigms as *closer or farther*, without a
metric — the shape of Asimov's *less wrong* (Section 5.3). Two questions then remain.
Closer to *what* Attractor, judged on whose criteria? And the system that converges
is here a community of observers, whose attractor is a way of knowing: in the
vocabulary of second-order cybernetics (cited for the record, not as a foundation —
`ContextSwitching.md`, §2), that would be a second-order use of a primitive defined
in first-order terms (Wiener, Ashby). Whether `L` admits that use as it stands is
open. A candidate, not a claim; not to be written into any formula.

**Plural anomalies.** Geocentrism suggests that `anomaly` may need to hold a
sequence. To be settled at graving.

**Where a history begins and ends.** What counts as "one question" — the boundary
of a ParadigmHistory — is the modeller's choice, and so ordinary modelling bias
(δ₁). No rule is proposed yet.

**The Territory side of the episode.** A paradigm is characterised on the Map and
Stereopsis side; the signatures of Section 5.4 nonetheless use the attractor (A),
a Territory primitive. Whether the historical episode itself is read with the
Territory grammar, and how far, is not settled.

**Graving.** The encoding of the consensus period (OWL-Time or dedicated
properties), the typing of the two combos and the domain declaration are for SC-7;
the form of declared criteria, if it is ever graved, goes through the
ontology-diagnosis pipeline once it has paid for itself by use. No facet for
paradigms is planned, until further notice. The User Guide is updated once the
declaration form is settled, not before.

**Seed coverage.** Three relations are under-seeded (Section 8.4). The type is not
sealed until they are not.

This document is itself provisional — a position at one date, revisable like every
document in this folder.
