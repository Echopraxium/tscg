## 1. The Idea

An **epistemic pivot** is an object around which several **modes of access** —
building, observing, experimenting, transmitting — turn while reaching the same
structure.

A pivot is the **fixed point** around which something turns. Here the object stays put
and the points of view turn around it. In geometry, the fixed point of a rotation is
literally the **invariant of the transformation** — the name carries Klein's programme
and `Note_InvariantsWithoutMetric_2026-10-22.md`. A knot is a pivot for the sailor
(tying it), the topologist (classifying it), the child (handling it), the storyteller
(the Gordian knot).

**Two senses to rule out.**
- *To pivot*, in everyday use, means to change direction (a start-up's pivot; the sudden
  belief shifts of "Pivoting Right", *Philosophy*, Cambridge). There the thing itself
  turns. Here the opposite holds: **the pivot stays fixed; the observer turns.**
- Wittgenstein's **hinges** (*On Certainty*, §341: some propositions are "as it were like
  hinges on which those turn" — our questions and doubts), today's *hinge epistemology*
  (D. Moyal-Sharrock, D. Pritchard, A. Coliva). "Pivot" will be heard as a cousin of
  "hinge". The difference: a hinge is a **proposition** exempt from doubt — close to
  Lakatos's hard core or Descartes's cogito (`CRATE.md` §1); a pivot is a **concrete
  object** reached by several modes and **open to examination**. Declared, the
  neighbourhood links this document to the rest of the crate.

Three questions define the notion:

1. **Which modes of access** reach the object?
2. **What is invariant** across those modes — what every mode agrees on?
3. **What does each mode lose** — what it cannot carry?

## 1b. Readers and Their Prisms (Michel, 2026-10-23)

What drew Michel to the name: a deliberate step back from academic or institutional
truth, toward **an object both simple and complex**, which anyone can appropriate
according to their culture, their experience and their level of reading — their
**prism**.

**Two axes around one pivot.**

| Axis | Varies | Example — the Möbius strip |
|---|---|---|
| **Modes of access** (Section 1) | *how* one reaches the object: build, observe, experiment, transmit | the same child makes it, traces its single side, cuts it |
| **Readers' prisms** | *who* reads it: culture, experience, level of reading | a child, a craftsman and a topologist all cut it, and read different things in the same result |

The pivot is the fixed point of both axes at once.

**"Simple and complex at once" has a name.** S. Papert, *Mindstorms* (1980):
**objects-to-think-with** — his founding example is the gears he played with as a child,
which later helped him understand equations. M. Resnick condenses the requirement for
learning tools into **low floor** (anyone can enter), **high ceiling** (one can go very
far) and **wide walls** (many paths). Wide walls are the readers' prisms. This is also
TSCG's declared vocation (the Lego Technic metaphor) — reassuring for the substance, and
one more alignment to watch (Section 5).

**The guard-rail: the object answers everyone the same way.** Stepping back from
institutional truth could slide into "to each their own truth". The pivot prevents it.
Cut a Möbius strip along its middle: it gives one loop — for the child, the craftsman and
the mathematician, whatever their culture. The result can be interpreted in many ways; it
cannot be negotiated. Authority does not vanish: it moves **from the institution to the
object** — the *nullius in verba* of `ContextSwitching.md` §8.2 (take nobody's word for
it; go and see). Readings vary with the prism; the invariant is the same for all. That is
what separates **appropriation** from **relativism**.

**Complement, not replace** (Michel: "both more accurate and more humble"). The pivot does
not substitute for academic knowledge; it offers a way in that needs no authorisation.
The topologist is not disqualified: she is one prism among others around the same strip —
the one with the highest ceiling. Claiming to *replace* would expose the document to the
opposite objection (anti-expertise), and would contradict its own method, which leans on
published parents.

## 2. Three Worked Objects

| Object | Build | Observe | Experiment | Transmit | Invariant across modes | Loss, by mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Möbius strip** | a paper band, half-twist, tape | one side, one edge (trace a line with a pencil) | cut along the middle: one loop twice as long, not two | a child can teach it to a child | one side, one edge — survives crumpled paper and crooked tape | the gesture does not carry *why* (non-orientability); the formula does not carry the surprise |
| **Trefoil knot** | tie it | count crossings | pull, twist — it never becomes an unknot without cutting | sailors' practice; tables; myth | the knot type, under "the rope is not cut" | the hand does not prove two knots differ; the proof does not tie anything |
| **π** | a circle and a string | unroll the string: about 3.1 | repeat with larger circles: the ratio holds | a gesture, a number, a series | the ratio circumference/diameter | the string gives an approximation, never irrationality; the series gives digits, never the circle |

The table makes one pattern visible: **structural invariants survive the imprecision of
the hand; measures do not** (`Note_InvariantsWithoutMetric_2026-10-22.md` §9).

## 3. Published Parents

| Parent | What it contributes | What it leaves out |
|---|---|---|
| **Boundary object** — S. L. Star & J. R. Griesemer, *Social Studies of Science* 19 (1989) | One object shared by several **communities**, each reading it its own way, while keeping its identity across them | Organised around communities rather than modes of access — but the second axis of Section 1b (readers and their prisms) brings it back as a **close** parent, not a distant one |
| **Trading zone** — P. Galison, *Image and Logic* (1997) | The **common language** (pidgin, then creole) that grows from exchange around an object | Organised around language, not the object |
| **Robustness as multiple accessibility** — W. C. Wimsatt, "Robustness, Reliability, and Overdetermination" (1981); M. I. Eronen, "Robustness and Reality", *Synthese* (2015) | What remains **invariant across several independent means of access** is, to that extent, real; Eronen argues this justifies ontological commitments | The closest parent; it says *why* several accesses matter, not how a learner uses them |
| **Mode and medium** — T. Knuuttila, models as epistemic artifacts (2005, 2011, 2017; see Knuuttila & Loettgers 2021) | The same structure in **different media** affords different inferences (the repressilator: mathematical, synthetic, electronic) | Centred on scientific models, not on everyday objects |
| **Modes of representation** — J. Bruner, *Toward a Theory of Instruction* (1966); **multiple representations** — S. Ainsworth, "DeFT", *Learning and Instruction* 16 (2006) | Enactive, iconic, symbolic; multiple representations serve distinct learning functions — and their benefits are "not always achieved" | Didactic; Ainsworth's caution is a cost this document must carry |
| **Triangulation** — N. K. Denzin, *The Research Act* (1970); in mixed-methods research, J. C. Greene, V. J. Caracelli & W. F. Graham, *Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis* 11(3) (1989) | One phenomenon approached through several methods, sources, investigators or theories, and the results compared. Greene et al. already distinguish purposes beyond convergence — complementarity, and *initiation*: seeking divergences to learn from them | Probably the **closest single parent** (found late, 2026-10-23). It covers questions 1 and 2, and partly 3; it is framed for research designs, not for concrete objects handled by learners |

**Name history — "epistemic interface", set aside (2026-10-23).** The first name was
already used: L. Marin, "'A Place
of very Arduous interfaces'. Social Media Platforms as Epistemic Environments with Faulty
Interfaces", *Topoi* 43(5), 1517–1527 (2024), defines an *epistemic interface* as "any
informational interface (be it technical, human or institutional)" through which epistemic
goods are transferred from one epistemic environment to its outside. That sense is
**different**: an interface *between environments* (a channel), not an *object with
several modes of access* (a hub). Michel chose to rename: "epistemic pivot".

**Closest parent for hand-held objects (found 2026-10-23).** M. Trybulec &
I. Iłowiecka-Tańska, "Are Interactive Exhibits at a Science Center Cognitive Artifacts?",
*Foundations of Science* 30, 651–675 (2025). Science-centre exhibits handled in "reality
mode" — played with, not read as representations — still perform cognitive functions
"even in the absence of a representational relation". They draw on R. Heersmink
(ecological cognitive artifacts) and L. Malafouris (*How Things Shape the Mind*, 2013:
the "enactive sign", which works "not for what it represents but for what it brings
forth"). This is the **build / experiment** side of the Möbius strip, already studied — a
parent this document must cite, and a ready out-of-sample test bed (Section 8b).

Classic illustration of Wimsatt's point: Jean Perrin's determination of Avogadro's number
by many independent methods (*Les Atomes*, 1913) — their agreement is what made atoms
accepted. Several modes of access around one pivot; their concordance as evidence.

**Status of novelty (2026-10-23).** This document does **not** claim an innovation.
Assessed honestly:

- **Not an original concept.** Each of the three questions of Section 1 already exists:
  several modes of access (Bruner, Ainsworth), the invariant across them (Wimsatt,
  Perrin, Whewell's consilience, triangulation), the loss per mode (Knuuttila;
  `ContextSwitching.md` §11; in part Greene et al.'s *initiation*).
- **Not a mere renaming** — as far as the search went, no single parent covers the three
  questions together: robustness serves to justify what is *real*; the boundary object is
  organised around *communities* (close to the readers' axis of Section 1b, not to the modes of access); triangulation is framed for *research designs*.
- **A synthesis.** What may be specific is the **assembly**: the three questions held
  together, applied to **concrete, hand-held objects**, for a **pedagogical** purpose,
  with the loss per mode recorded as a result in its own right.

A synthesis counts as an innovation only if the assembly lets one see or do something
that the parts, taken separately, do not. That is **not shown yet**; it is what Section 8b
is for. Two further limits: the search was partial (no Google Scholar; two parents —
Marin and triangulation — were found late), so a close synthesis may exist elsewhere;
and novelty is for others to judge, not for the toolkit that helped it emerge. Rule kept
for this document (Michel): **better to be legitimate without claiming it than to claim it
without being legitimate.**

## 4. Relation to TSCG

- **Map–Territory (CH1).** Each mode of access is a Map; the object sits on the
  Territory side. An epistemic pivot is a place where several Maps of one Territory
  can be compared, and where their agreement can be checked.
- **Context switching.** Moving between modes of access is a switch of context; the
  third question (what each mode loses) is the rule of `ContextSwitching.md` §11 applied
  to one object.
- **Rediscovery over recitation** (`ContextSwitching.md` §8). An epistemic pivot is
  an object that lets a learner rediscover rather than recite — the Möbius strip is the
  *Meno*'s square drawn in the sand.
- **Bachelard.** His suspicion targets *first* experience; an epistemic pivot is a
  *constructed* experience. Bachelard: « les instruments ne sont que des théories
  matérialisées » (*Le Nouvel Esprit scientifique*, 1934; PUF ed. 2013, p. 17 — first in
  "Noumène et microphysique", 1931–32, repr. in *Études*, Vrin, 1970) — his
  *phénoménotechnique*. Verified 2026-10-23 via secondary sources citing the page.

## 5. Dogfooding Risk — Named Up Front

The four modes fall almost exactly on REVOI: build → `R`, observe → `O`,
experiment → `V`, transmit → `Im`. **This is not a confirmation.** REVOI was designed to
describe how a system is known; it is no surprise that it fits a list of ways of knowing.
Using TSCG to confirm TSCG is the failure this crate names *epistemic dogfooding*. No
claim in this document rests on that fit.

## 6. Guard-Rails

- **The object is concrete; its reading is not.** Seeing two knots as "the same" needs
  the declared criterion "the rope is not cut". An epistemic pivot works only once its
  **equivalence criterion is declared** (Klein: an invariant exists only relative to a
  group of transformations).
- **Agreement is not truth.** Concordant modes of access can share a hidden bias; Wimsatt
  requires the means of access to be *independent*, and independence must be argued, not
  assumed.
- **Multiplying modes has a cost** (Ainsworth): a learner must also translate between
  them. More modes is not automatically better.
- **No new pieces in the kit.** This document adds no M2 concept and no property.

## 8. Where the Regress Stops

Michel's motive (2026-10-23): to get out of the **infinite recursion of truth**. The
recursion has a name — the **Münchhausen trilemma** (H. Albert, *Traktat über kritische
Vernunft*, 1968), after Agrippa's trilemma reported by Sextus Empiricus. Any attempt to
justify a claim ends in one of three ways: **infinite regress** (each justification calls
for another — "turtles all the way down"), **circularity** (the Cartesian circle), or a
**dogmatic stop** (a point declared beyond question).

**The pivot does not escape the trilemma; it changes where the regress stops.**

| Where the regress stops | Who | Kind of stop |
|---|---|---|
| a self-evident axiom | Descartes (the cogito) | a certainty that claims to be demonstrated |
| a decision | Popper (piles in a swamp), Lakatos (the hard core) | a provisional, declared choice |
| an authority | the institution | taking someone's word for it |
| **a gesture on an object** | **the pivot** | **public, reproducible, open to anyone** |

L. Wittgenstein said it first, *On Certainty* §204: giving grounds comes to an end, but
the end is not propositions that strike us as immediately true — it is **our acting**.
One stops asking "why is it true?" when one can cut the strip and look.

The pivot still has its turtle: its equivalence criterion ("the rope is not cut"). The
difference is that this turtle can be **touched** — it is not hidden under the disc but
on the table, where anyone can examine it. The regress is not escaped; it is stopped at a
place that is visible, checkable and shared, rather than at one that requires taking
someone's word. That is the most that can honestly be claimed.
