One parameter: Δθ, degrees per frame

The frame is the sampler. Everything depends on how far the disc turns between two frames — Δθ, swept automatically from 0° to 360° and looping.

Prove the patterns are in the eye

Press ⏸ Stop: the rings and rays vanish and a plain yin-yang reappears. The figure never changed — the patterns come from integrating successive frames, not from the disc. Press Speed Scan to switch to Select Speed and hold any Δθ.

Because of the disc's order-2 symmetry, the whole ladder of patterns repeats twice across 0–360°.

How it renders

Each frame the disc advances by Δθ. The display shows the average of the last 8 frames — a persistence-of-vision window. When Δθ ≈ 180°/q, those frames land on q distinct orientations → q superimposed yin-yangs, with grey levels in steps of 1/q. Away from such ratios the copies spread → a grey wash.

Territory reference

The outer tick shows the true rotation direction. Its on-screen speed is capped so the reference itself doesn't alias on your monitor.

Honest limits

  • "Per frame" means your browser's frame — 60, 120 or 144 Hz. The patterns depend only on Δθ, so they match; their scroll speed may not.
  • The yin-yang is angularly smooth, so high-q figures read mainly through the two dots and the S-curve edges.
