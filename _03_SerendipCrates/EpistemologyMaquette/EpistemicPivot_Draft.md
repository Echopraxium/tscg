## 1. The Idea

An **epistemic pivot** is an object around which several **modes of access** —
building, observing, experimenting, transmitting — turn while reaching the same
structure.

A pivot is the **fixed point** around which something turns. Here the object stays put
and the points of view turn around it. In geometry, the fixed point of a rotation is
literally the **invariant of the transformation** — the name carries Klein's programme
and `Note_InvariantsWithoutMetric_2026-10-22.md`. A knot is a pivot for the sailor
(tying it), the topologist (classifying it), the child (handling it), the storyteller
(the Gordian knot).

**Two senses to rule out.**
- *To pivot*, in everyday use, means to change direction (a start-up's pivot; the sudden
  belief shifts of "Pivoting Right", *Philosophy*, Cambridge). There the thing itself
  turns. Here the opposite holds: **the pivot stays fixed; the observer turns.**
- Wittgenstein's **hinges** (*On Certainty*, §341: some propositions are "as it were like
  hinges on which those turn" — our questions and doubts), today's *hinge epistemology*
  (D. Moyal-Sharrock, D. Pritchard, A. Coliva). "Pivot" will be heard as a cousin of
  "hinge". The difference: a hinge is a **proposition** exempt from doubt — close to
  Lakatos's hard core or Descartes's cogito (`CRATE.md` §1); a pivot is a **concrete
  object** reached by several modes and **open to examination**. Declared, the
  neighbourhood links this draft to the rest of the crate.

Three questions define the notion:

1. **Which modes of access** reach the object?
2. **What is invariant** across those modes — what every mode agrees on?
3. **What does each mode lose** — what it cannot carry?

## 2. Three Worked Objects

| Object | Build | Observe | Experiment | Transmit | Invariant across modes | Loss, by mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Möbius strip** | a paper band, half-twist, tape | one side, one edge (trace a line with a pencil) | cut along the middle: one loop twice as long, not two | a child can teach it to a child | one side, one edge — survives crumpled paper and crooked tape | the gesture does not carry *why* (non-orientability); the formula does not carry the surprise |
| **Trefoil knot** | tie it | count crossings | pull, twist — it never becomes an unknot without cutting | sailors' practice; tables; myth | the knot type, under "the rope is not cut" | the hand does not prove two knots differ; the proof does not tie anything |
| **π** | a circle and a string | unroll the string: about 3.1 | repeat with larger circles: the ratio holds | a gesture, a number, a series | the ratio circumference/diameter | the string gives an approximation, never irrationality; the series gives digits, never the circle |

The table makes one pattern visible: **structural invariants survive the imprecision of
the hand; measures do not** (`Note_InvariantsWithoutMetric_2026-10-22.md` §9).

## 3. Published Parents

| Parent | What it contributes | What it leaves out |
|---|---|---|
| **Boundary object** — S. L. Star & J. R. Griesemer, *Social Studies of Science* 19 (1989) | One object shared by several **communities**, each reading it its own way, while keeping its identity across them | Organised around communities, not modes of access |
| **Trading zone** — P. Galison, *Image and Logic* (1997) | The **common language** (pidgin, then creole) that grows from exchange around an object | Organised around language, not the object |
| **Robustness as multiple accessibility** — W. C. Wimsatt, "Robustness, Reliability, and Overdetermination" (1981); M. I. Eronen, "Robustness and Reality", *Synthese* (2015) | What remains **invariant across several independent means of access** is, to that extent, real; Eronen argues this justifies ontological commitments | The closest parent; it says *why* several accesses matter, not how a learner uses them |
| **Mode and medium** — T. Knuuttila, models as epistemic artifacts (2005, 2011, 2017; see Knuuttila & Loettgers 2021) | The same structure in **different media** affords different inferences (the repressilator: mathematical, synthetic, electronic) | Centred on scientific models, not on everyday objects |
| **Modes of representation** — J. Bruner, *Toward a Theory of Instruction* (1966); **multiple representations** — S. Ainsworth, "DeFT", *Learning and Instruction* 16 (2006) | Enactive, iconic, symbolic; multiple representations serve distinct learning functions — and their benefits are "not always achieved" | Didactic; Ainsworth's caution is a cost this draft must carry |

**Name history — "epistemic interface", set aside (2026-10-23).** The first name was
already used: L. Marin, "'A Place
of very Arduous interfaces'. Social Media Platforms as Epistemic Environments with Faulty
Interfaces", *Topoi* 43(5), 1517–1527 (2024), defines an *epistemic interface* as "any
informational interface (be it technical, human or institutional)" through which epistemic
goods are transferred from one epistemic environment to its outside. That sense is
**different**: an interface *between environments* (a channel), not an *object with
several modes of access* (a hub). Michel chose to rename: "epistemic pivot".

**Closest parent for hand-held objects (found 2026-10-23).** M. Trybulec &
I. Iłowiecka-Tańska, "Are Interactive Exhibits at a Science Center Cognitive Artifacts?",
*Foundations of Science* 30, 651–675 (2025). Science-centre exhibits handled in "reality
mode" — played with, not read as representations — still perform cognitive functions
"even in the absence of a representational relation". They draw on R. Heersmink
(ecological cognitive artifacts) and L. Malafouris (*How Things Shape the Mind*, 2013:
the "enactive sign", which works "not for what it represents but for what it brings
forth"). This is the **build / experiment** side of the Möbius strip, already studied — a
parent this draft must cite, and a ready out-of-sample test bed (Section 8).

Classic illustration of Wimsatt's point: Jean Perrin's determination of Avogadro's number
by many independent methods (*Les Atomes*, 1913) — their agreement is what made atoms
accepted. Several modes of access around one pivot; their concordance as evidence.

**What the draft claims to add.** No parent, as far as the search went, joins the three
questions of Section 1: modes of access (not communities), the invariant across them
(Wimsatt), and the loss per mode (Knuuttila; `ContextSwitching.md` §11). That joining is
the whole of the claim.

## 4. Relation to TSCG

- **Map–Territory (CH1).** Each mode of access is a Map; the object sits on the
  Territory side. An epistemic pivot is a place where several Maps of one Territory
  can be compared, and where their agreement can be checked.
- **Context switching.** Moving between modes of access is a switch of context; the
  third question (what each mode loses) is the rule of `ContextSwitching.md` §11 applied
  to one object.
- **Rediscovery over recitation** (`ContextSwitching.md` §8). An epistemic pivot is
  an object that lets a learner rediscover rather than recite — the Möbius strip is the
  *Meno*'s square drawn in the sand.
- **Bachelard.** His suspicion targets *first* experience; an epistemic pivot is a
  *constructed* experience. Bachelard: « les instruments ne sont que des théories
  matérialisées » (*Le Nouvel Esprit scientifique*, 1934; PUF ed. 2013, p. 17 — first in
  "Noumène et microphysique", 1931–32, repr. in *Études*, Vrin, 1970) — his
  *phénoménotechnique*. Verified 2026-10-23 via secondary sources citing the page.

## 5. Dogfooding Risk — Named Up Front

The four modes fall almost exactly on REVOI: build → `R`, observe → `O`,
experiment → `V`, transmit → `Im`. **This is not a confirmation.** REVOI was designed to
describe how a system is known; it is no surprise that it fits a list of ways of knowing.
Using TSCG to confirm TSCG is the failure this crate names *epistemic dogfooding*. No
claim in this draft rests on that fit.

## 6. Guard-Rails

- **The object is concrete; its reading is not.** Seeing two knots as "the same" needs
  the declared criterion "the rope is not cut". An epistemic pivot works only once its
  **equivalence criterion is declared** (Klein: an invariant exists only relative to a
  group of transformations).
- **Agreement is not truth.** Concordant modes of access can share a hidden bias; Wimsatt
  requires the means of access to be *independent*, and independence must be argued, not
  assumed.
- **Multiplying modes has a cost** (Ainsworth): a learner must also translate between
  them. More modes is not automatically better.
- **No new pieces in the kit.** This draft adds no M2 concept and no property.

## 8. Before Graduation — Paid by Use

The draft moves to `docs/CoreHypotheses/` only if at least one of these is done and
passes:

1. **A maquette.** A minimal M0 instance (e.g. the Möbius strip) built through the four
   modes, showing something a prose description blurs — the anti-decorative test of
   `ModelSupersession.md` §8.
2. **An out-of-sample test.** Take a boundary object studied **by others** — the
   specimens of Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology in Star & Griesemer (1989) — and
   check whether its modes of access can be described with the three questions of
   Section 1 **without forcing**, and whether the REVOI fit survives or breaks.

If neither passes, the draft stays in the crate, or is marked `abandoned` with its reason.

## 9. The Shore

- Is "mode of access" one category, or several (gesture, perception, instrument, sign)?
- Are the four modes complete? Is *contemplating*, *narrating* or *playing* a fifth?
- How is the independence of two modes argued (Wimsatt's condition)?
- **Name.** "Epistemic pivot" (Michel, 2026-10-23). Search record: web, PhilPapers /
  PhilArchive, Springer; extended searches across museum studies, design, archaeology,
  science education, anthropology, interdisciplinarity. No established use of
  "epistemic pivot" found; "epistemic interface" set aside (Marin, 2024). Google Scholar
  could not be queried from this environment (robots disallowed) — a manual pass by
  Michel remains advisable.
