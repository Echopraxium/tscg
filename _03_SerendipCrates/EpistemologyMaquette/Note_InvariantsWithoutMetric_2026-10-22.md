## 1. Origin

A chain of four images proposed by Michel during the feasibility session on the
Lakatos maquette:

1. **The prism.** Every observer looks through a prism — unavoidably, structurally.
   Some prisms distort less (glass vs diamond). The vacuum, like silence, is a limit
   concept no observer occupies.
2. **Relativity.** Could relativity be extended to epistemology?
3. **Topological invariants.** What survives even very strong deformations?
4. **Structural grammar.** Invariants without a metric, as in the migration of M3 from
   "tensor spaces" to the structural grammar.

Taken together, they form one idea: **what survives a change of perspective, once we
give up a privileged frame and a common metric?**

## 2. What each image contributes

| Image | What it says | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Prism** | Distortion is unavoidable. Within one criterion it can be graded: *less distorting*. Distortion is also what produces information: a prism disperses white light into a spectrum, which vacuum never shows. What distorts is not the slowing down but the **unequal treatment** of components (dispersion, index gradients) | Grading needs a reference — the vacuum — that no one occupies |
| **Relativity** | No privileged frame; but a **translation rule** between frames (Lorentz) and **invariants** all observers agree on (c, the spacetime interval, proper time). Not relativism: Einstein reportedly regretted the name, saying "theory of invariants" would have fitted better | Relativity was born from rejecting a reference medium (the luminiferous ether — a `ModelSupersession.md` seed). It conflicts with the prism's vacuum unless the two images answer different questions |
| **Topology / Erlangen** | Felix Klein (1872): a geometry is defined by a group of transformations and what it leaves invariant. The more transformations allowed, the fewer invariants survive, but the more robust they are (Euclidean → affine → projective → topological) | In epistemology, nobody knows what a *continuous* transformation between paradigms would be |
| **Structural grammar** | The step the M3 reform already took: drop the metric, keep the structure (below) | Formulated with TSCG's own tools — mirror risk |

Proposed division of labour (reading **(b)**, agreed 2026-10-22):

- **the prism** grades distortion *within* one declared criterion;
- **relativity** translates *between* criteria, when a translation rule exists;
- **structural invariants** are what may survive even when no fine translation exists.

A ranking between paradigms is legitimate only when the common criterion is
**declared**. Where it cannot be declared (Kuhn: criteria change across a revolution),
there is no ranking. `ContextSwitching.md` §3.5 and `ModelSupersession.md` §3.5 are
**not** amended now: the nuance must first pay for itself by use (the Lakatos maquette
ranks programmes within one criterion — novel predictions confirmed).

## 3. Anchor on HEAD — the structural grammar already has two levels

From `ontology/StructuralGrammar/Structural_Grammar_Foundation.md`:

- §4: the structural grammar does **not** presuppose a normed space and does **not**
  require an inner product or orthonormality. It keeps a free commutative monoid over
  primitive types, the operators `×` (Gt), `+` (Gm), `|` (Gs), a Lambek / Curry–Howard
  reading, and functors.
- §6: **M3/M2 carry no metric; M0 does** (dimensions as evaluation functions into [0,1]).
- §7: δ₁ is a normalised Euclidean distance at M0, and also has a **structural**
  reading: a measure of the non-isomorphism of Φ between Map and Territory.

So TSCG already separates:

| Level | Metric? | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| M3 / M2 | No | structural invariants (formulas, signatures) |
| M0 | Yes | scores, δ₁ — meaningful only within a declared evaluation (the prism) |

The transdisciplinary bet rests on the first row: one M2 formula instantiated in
several domains, with different scores but **the same signature**.

**Precision.** "Without a metric" is not "topological". Topology needs neighbourhoods
or continuity; a monoid has none. The invariants the grammar keeps are **algebraic /
categorical** — preserved by functors. The right family is category theory, which
Eilenberg and Mac Lane (1945) presented as a continuation of Klein's Erlangen
programme.

## 4. The hypothesis

Kuhn's incommensurability bears on the **meaning** of concepts (Newtonian vs
relativistic *mass*). It may not bear on their **structure of composition**.

> **H3.** Between two historically recorded paradigms, the invariant is neither a
> universal constant nor a topological invariant, but a **shared structural signature**.

H3 may be false: two paradigms can have different signatures.

## 5. External parents (published, dated)

- F. Klein, *Erlanger Programm* (1872).
- E. Cassirer, *Zur Einsteinschen Relativitätstheorie* (1921) — objectivity as
  invariance under change of frame.
- S. Eilenberg & S. Mac Lane, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences" (1945).
- R. Nozick, *Invariances: The Structure of the Objective World* (2001).
- Background examples: C. F. Gauss, *Theorema Egregium* (1827) — no flat map of the
  sphere preserves distances; H. Beck, London Underground diagram (1931) — a map that
  keeps only connections and order.

A maquette would model **one of these** (e.g. Nozick or Cassirer) as an external
epistemology — never H3 as TSCG's own thesis.

## 6. Failure test (stated before building)

- **Forcing.** Can two historically recorded paradigms be shown to share a structural
  signature **without arbitrarily choosing** what counts as "the same structure"?
  If the shared signature has to be imposed, H3 fails.
- **Mirror.** If every correspondence found is one TSCG had already written
  (`m3:Functor`, `survivingInvariant`, context switching), the maquette is relabelling.
- **Decorative mapping to avoid.** "Bounding by morphism = continuous deformation;
  refutation = tearing; restoration = regluing" is tempting and may be projected, not
  revealed (the synesthetic corner of `The_Epistemic_Residue.md`). Not to be used unless
  continuity between paradigms is defined first.

## 9. Addendum 2026-10-23 — Form as a reference frame (Michel)

**Proposal.** The "reference frame" between paradigms may be **form and its structure**,
made concrete by hand, without theory: the art of knots, π by unrolling a string along a
circle, the Möbius strip made from a paper band. These are pragmatic, eloquent,
pedagogical illustrations of topology and geometry.

**What makes it strong — structural invariants survive the imprecision of the hand;
measures do not.**

- π with a string gives roughly 3.1, depending on care; no string shows that π is
  irrational. (Precision: with radius 1 the unrolled string measures 2π; a diameter of 1
  gives π.)
- A loosely tied trefoil is still a trefoil; however it is pulled, as long as the rope is
  not cut, it never becomes an unknot or a figure-eight.
- A Möbius strip made with crumpled paper and crooked tape still has one side and one
  edge.

This is §3 of this note made tangible: the structure survives a clumsy gesture; the
metric does not. A child can check it.

**The hand discovers before theory.** Cutting a Möbius strip along its middle gives one
loop twice as long, not two — almost nobody predicts it, anybody can observe it
(`ContextSwitching.md` §8, rediscovery over recitation; Plato's *Meno*, the square drawn
in the sand). History adds a fitting case: mathematical knot theory grew out of an
abandoned physical model — Kelvin's vortex atom (1867). Tait compiled knot tables to
classify atoms; the vortex atom disappeared, the tables remained. A refuted paradigm left
an invariant behind.

**Bachelard need not object.** His suspicion targets *first* experience, the one that
goes without saying. A Möbius strip is a *constructed* experience. His own phrase
(*Le Nouvel Esprit scientifique*, 1934; PUF ed. 2013, p. 17): « les instruments ne sont
que des théories matérialisées » — here, topology materialised in paper. Verified
2026-10-23.

**A candidate answer to "what is the c of epistemology?"** The knot exists for the
sailor, in Inca quipus, in myth (the Gordian knot) and for the mathematician — each reads
it by its own criteria, all point at the same object. S. L. Star & J. R. Griesemer (1989)
called this a **boundary object**: plastic enough to serve each community's needs, robust
enough to keep its identity across them. Not a universal constant, but **shared objects
that different contexts can agree to point at**.

**Limit.** The object is concrete; its *reading* is not. Seeing two deformed knots as
"the same" already requires the criterion "the rope is not cut" (ambient isotopy). A
child who has not adopted it sees two different tangles. A form becomes a reference
frame only once its **equivalence criterion is declared** — Klein again: an invariant
exists only relative to a chosen group of transformations. Form does not remove the
turtle; it makes it visible and manipulable.

## 10. Caution — epistemic dogfooding (Michel, 2026-10-23)

**Beware of epistemic dogfooding**: using TSCG to confirm TSCG. The form-as-reference
idea aligns remarkably well with what TSCG already is (a construction kit, maquettes, a
pedagogical vocation) — and so did almost every idea in this crate's sessions. That
convergence is an alarm signal, not a confirmation (criterion 5 of `CRATE.md`; TSCG can
be neither self-validated nor self-refuted, `ModelSupersession.md`). Every seed here is
to be tested out-of-sample and judged by others, never by the kit itself.

## 11. Addendum 2026-10-23 — "Epistemic interface", renamed "epistemic pivot" (Michel)

> **Renamed.** The term below was first named *epistemic interface*; after a name
> collision (Marin, *Topoi*, 2024) Michel renamed it **epistemic pivot**. The text keeps
> the first wording as a record; the current text is `docs/CoreHypotheses/EpistemicPivot.md`.

**Proposal.** One object, many ways to **build** it, **observe** it, **experiment** with
it, and **transmit** the knowledge of it. Could such an object be called an *epistemic
interface*? The structure is a star: the object at the centre, modes of knowing plugged
into it. In the software sense, an interface is the surface where two systems exchange;
one object can expose several interfaces without changing. The knot exposes one to the
sailor (tying it), to the topologist (classifying it), to the child (handling it), to the
storyteller (the Gordian knot).

**Close parents — they do not say quite the same thing.**

| Concept | Centred on |
|---|---|
| **Boundary object** — S. L. Star & J. R. Griesemer, "Institutional Ecology, 'Translations' and Boundary Objects", *Social Studies of Science* (1989) | the **communities** that share an object while each reads it its own way |
| **Trading zone** — P. Galison, *Image and Logic* (1997) | the **common language** (pidgin, then creole) that scientific subcultures build to exchange around an object |
| **Epistemic thing** — H.-J. Rheinberger, *Toward a History of Epistemic Things* (1997) | the object **still under investigation**, its nature open, as opposed to the stabilised technical object |

Literature search (2026-10-23): the term is used by L. Marin (*Topoi*, 2024) in a
different sense — an interface *between epistemic environments*. See
`docs/CoreHypotheses/EpistemicPivot.md` §3 (name history).

**What the term could add.** The parents are organised around *communities* or the
*status* of the object. "Epistemic interface" would be organised around **modes of
access** — build, observe, experiment, transmit — and the question of **what each mode
preserves or loses** (the string around the circle transmits π as a gesture, not its
irrationality).

**Dogfooding risk, named up front.** The four verbs fall almost exactly on REVOI:
build → `R`, observe → `O`, experiment → `V`, transmit → `Im`. That is the too-easy fit
§10 warns against: REVOI was designed to describe how a system is known, so it is no
surprise that it fits a list of ways of knowing. This is **not** a confirmation.

**Out-of-sample test.** Take a boundary object studied **by others** — e.g. the specimens
of Berkeley's Museum of Vertebrate Zoology in Star & Griesemer (1989) — and check whether
the modes of access they describe can be sorted into REVOI **without forcing**. If some
mode has no home, or several modes collapse into one dimension, the correspondence is
partial and must be declared as such.
