## 1. Origin

A chain of four images proposed by Michel during the feasibility session on the
Lakatos maquette:

1. **The prism.** Every observer looks through a prism — unavoidably, structurally.
   Some prisms distort less (glass vs diamond). The vacuum, like silence, is a limit
   concept no observer occupies.
2. **Relativity.** Could relativity be extended to epistemology?
3. **Topological invariants.** What survives even very strong deformations?
4. **Structural grammar.** Invariants without a metric, as in the migration of M3 from
   "tensor spaces" to the structural grammar.

Taken together, they form one idea: **what survives a change of perspective, once we
give up a privileged frame and a common metric?**

## 2. What each image contributes

| Image | What it says | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Prism** | Distortion is unavoidable. Within one criterion it can be graded: *less distorting*. Distortion is also what produces information: a prism disperses white light into a spectrum, which vacuum never shows. What distorts is not the slowing down but the **unequal treatment** of components (dispersion, index gradients) | Grading needs a reference — the vacuum — that no one occupies |
| **Relativity** | No privileged frame; but a **translation rule** between frames (Lorentz) and **invariants** all observers agree on (c, the spacetime interval, proper time). Not relativism: Einstein reportedly regretted the name, saying "theory of invariants" would have fitted better | Relativity was born from rejecting a reference medium (the luminiferous ether — a `ModelSupersession.md` seed). It conflicts with the prism's vacuum unless the two images answer different questions |
| **Topology / Erlangen** | Felix Klein (1872): a geometry is defined by a group of transformations and what it leaves invariant. The more transformations allowed, the fewer invariants survive, but the more robust they are (Euclidean → affine → projective → topological) | In epistemology, nobody knows what a *continuous* transformation between paradigms would be |
| **Structural grammar** | The step the M3 reform already took: drop the metric, keep the structure (below) | Formulated with TSCG's own tools — mirror risk |

Proposed division of labour (reading **(b)**, agreed 2026-10-22):

- **the prism** grades distortion *within* one declared criterion;
- **relativity** translates *between* criteria, when a translation rule exists;
- **structural invariants** are what may survive even when no fine translation exists.

A ranking between paradigms is legitimate only when the common criterion is
**declared**. Where it cannot be declared (Kuhn: criteria change across a revolution),
there is no ranking. `ContextSwitching.md` §3.5 and `ModelSupersession.md` §3.5 are
**not** amended now: the nuance must first pay for itself by use (the Lakatos maquette
ranks programmes within one criterion — novel predictions confirmed).

## 3. Anchor on HEAD — the structural grammar already has two levels

From `ontology/StructuralGrammar/Structural_Grammar_Foundation.md`:

- §4: the structural grammar does **not** presuppose a normed space and does **not**
  require an inner product or orthonormality. It keeps a free commutative monoid over
  primitive types, the operators `×` (Gt), `+` (Gm), `|` (Gs), a Lambek / Curry–Howard
  reading, and functors.
- §6: **M3/M2 carry no metric; M0 does** (dimensions as evaluation functions into [0,1]).
- §7: δ₁ is a normalised Euclidean distance at M0, and also has a **structural**
  reading: a measure of the non-isomorphism of Φ between Map and Territory.

So TSCG already separates:

| Level | Metric? | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| M3 / M2 | No | structural invariants (formulas, signatures) |
| M0 | Yes | scores, δ₁ — meaningful only within a declared evaluation (the prism) |

The transdisciplinary bet rests on the first row: one M2 formula instantiated in
several domains, with different scores but **the same signature**.

**Precision.** "Without a metric" is not "topological". Topology needs neighbourhoods
or continuity; a monoid has none. The invariants the grammar keeps are **algebraic /
categorical** — preserved by functors. The right family is category theory, which
Eilenberg and Mac Lane (1945) presented as a continuation of Klein's Erlangen
programme.

## 4. The hypothesis

Kuhn's incommensurability bears on the **meaning** of concepts (Newtonian vs
relativistic *mass*). It may not bear on their **structure of composition**.

> **H3.** Between two historically recorded paradigms, the invariant is neither a
> universal constant nor a topological invariant, but a **shared structural signature**.

H3 may be false: two paradigms can have different signatures.

## 5. External parents (published, dated)

- F. Klein, *Erlanger Programm* (1872).
- E. Cassirer, *Zur Einsteinschen Relativitätstheorie* (1921) — objectivity as
  invariance under change of frame.
- S. Eilenberg & S. Mac Lane, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences" (1945).
- R. Nozick, *Invariances: The Structure of the Objective World* (2001).
- Background examples: C. F. Gauss, *Theorema Egregium* (1827) — no flat map of the
  sphere preserves distances; H. Beck, London Underground diagram (1931) — a map that
  keeps only connections and order.

A maquette would model **one of these** (e.g. Nozick or Cassirer) as an external
epistemology — never H3 as TSCG's own thesis.

## 6. Failure test (stated before building)

- **Forcing.** Can two historically recorded paradigms be shown to share a structural
  signature **without arbitrarily choosing** what counts as "the same structure"?
  If the shared signature has to be imposed, H3 fails.
- **Mirror.** If every correspondence found is one TSCG had already written
  (`m3:Functor`, `survivingInvariant`, context switching), the maquette is relabelling.
- **Decorative mapping to avoid.** "Bounding by morphism = continuous deformation;
  refutation = tearing; restoration = regluing" is tempting and may be projected, not
  revealed (the synesthetic corner of `The_Epistemic_Residue.md`). Not to be used unless
  continuity between paradigms is defined first.
