World 2D Projection

Projecting the round Earth onto a flat sheet is the Territory → Map relation made literal — the single distinction the whole TSCG framework rests on. The globe below is the Territory (read through ASFID); each 2D projection above is a Map (read through REVOI).

The poclet classifies five canonical projections along two orthogonal cartographic facets, and proves the irreducible gap between globe and map.

Two facets

distortionProperty Conformal · EqualArea · Equidistant · Compromise
graticuleGeometry Cylindrical · Conic · Azimuthal · Pseudocylindrical

The five anchors

  • Mercator — Conformal · Cylindrical (angles kept; poles explode)
  • Mollweide — EqualArea · Pseudocylindrical (areas exact; shapes shear)
  • Azimuthal Equidistant — Equidistant · Azimuthal (distance from centre exact)
  • Robinson — Compromise · Pseudocylindrical (nothing exact; looks right)
  • Gall-Peters — EqualArea · Cylindrical (orthogonality proof)

The gap is a theorem

Theorema Egregium (Gauss, 1827). Gaussian curvature is invariant under any distance-preserving map. The sphere has K = 1/R² > 0; the plane has K = 0. No isometry between them can exist — so every projection must distort.

The map can be made excellent along one chosen axis (angle, or area, or distance), yet can never equal the Territory. δ₁ = Liminal is not a defect — it is the lesson.
